# Settle and explain the budget

The advertiser should leave knowing what their money buys, how sure the number is and what they can choose.

- The amount and cadence come from the advertiser or an estimate they accept. Turning a monthly total into a daily budget is a new decision, and an amount in another currency should be asked for again in the account's currency rather than converted from memory.

- If the estimate returns no amount, say so and ask for a budget rather than supplying one. The strategy waits for a settled budget.

## Talk through the estimate

- Lead with a gap: when their amount is far below what the plan needs, say so early, with what each amount projects.

- Separate recommended from required. A learning floor or default is a recommendation, and starting smaller is fair. An account minimum is a hard limit the campaign can't run below.

- Offer levers beyond a bigger number: a more frequent event, fewer groups sharing the budget or a longer run.

- Match confidence to the source. A new account's estimate is a benchmark that sharpens once the campaign runs, history is quoted with its sample, and a cost that couldn't be estimated is unknown.

- Show projected results, not only money. Use one estimate, and price another setup only when asked.

- Their stated amount stays theirs. A bigger amount buying more is information, not pressure.
