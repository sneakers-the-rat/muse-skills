# Plan a campaign

Planning turns the request into a strategy the advertiser approves. It settles the account and identity, the destination and objective, what's optimized for and how it's tracked, regulated-category rules, geography and audience, structure and where the ads run, and budget and schedule. Each should rest on stated intent, evidence or a default shown as an assumption. If the advertiser already settled them all, that's the approved strategy and planning moves to creative.

Planning ends in a [binding-constraint](#resolve-a-binding-constraint) choice, one open question or the [complete plan](#recommend-the-complete-plan), even for a plan-only request, rather than an informational summary.

## What a good strategy looks like

- **Destination and result.** The destination, tracking, objective and optimized result are one decision: a result the destination can produce, tracking can measure and the objective supports. The destination should be somewhere the advertiser acts on results, such as a converting site, a form, chats they answer or calls they take, and the landing page should work. The plan should optimize for and promise only what tracking can measure. An unmeasured event is a binding constraint, and the plan should offer what can be measured instead.

- **Geography.** It should come from the advertiser or verified service-area evidence, not the account's currency, time zone, profile, domain or past campaigns.

- **Audience.** It should be the people the advertiser serves, with age, gender, interests and exclusions coming from them, not the business type. The plan should describe it only as its targeting encodes it. Personas shape creative, not targeting.

- **Structure.** One objective per campaign. Ads should be grouped separately only for real differences in budget, audience, geography, schedule or optimization, and a small budget kept to as few groups and tests as it can support. Several ads in one group aren't a controlled test, so a clean comparison needs the platform's experiment tool.

- **Budget and schedule.** They should fit the advertiser's limit and timing, priced as [budget](budget.md) describes. A goal that ends on a date should get an end date, offered as a backstop when the advertiser plans to stop it themselves.

## Gather what the strategy needs

- The brief is what only the advertiser knows that the strategy depends on. Ask for missing context together, with a short reason. Budget can be left open. Everything else comes from research, and the strategy is research's to recommend.

- A plan-only request gets the same research. Research is done when every source that matters has returned or been recorded unavailable. Names in a listing are leads, not evidence, and missing evidence leaves a setting open rather than choosing a replacement.

- Evidence should repair what it can, leaving at most one advertiser-owned blocker as the only question, asked once research is done.

- The strategy can name how many ads structure or pricing needs. Format, concept, copy and production belong to [creative](creative.md) after approval.

## Resolve a binding constraint

A binding constraint is evidence that makes the request unbuildable as asked, or splits it into materially different outcomes, targets or spend, such as an unmeasured event or one costing more than the advertiser's limit. The advertiser chooses the path, shown with the evidence and one recommendation, before the complete plan. The choice settles only that path: any value it needs comes next, affected research reruns, and the final structure is priced once.

## Recommend the complete plan

- The plan should lead with the recommendation, not a research log: business language for a beginner, setting names for an experienced advertiser.

- Each setting gets its value and a short basis, with the two to four findings that changed decisions, first-party first. State only assumptions or gaps that bound the plan. URLs, spending limits, offers, claims and service geography come from the advertiser or evidence.

- Approval accepts every shown value and leads to [creative](creative.md) without repeating research. A revision reruns affected research, reprices once if needed and shows the whole revised plan.

## Questions and options

- The complete plan asks "Proceed with this strategy?" with the options `Approve this strategy` and `No, make changes`.

- A binding-constraint choice offers the recommendation first, then the requested outcome while it's buildable, then a spend or target change that resolves it.
