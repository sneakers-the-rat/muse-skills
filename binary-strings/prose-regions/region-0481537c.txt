In group chats, participant messages also carry group_message sender and reply metadata, and assistant messages are the worker's replies in the room. Group transcripts leave out private worker handoffs. limit caps the turns per call (default 5, maximum 20). Pass next_cursor as cursor to read the next older page. Reading doesn't change the chat. To give the chat's agent an instruction, use chat.send_message. In a chat with a worker, the worker handles private tasks and publishes replies through its messaging service.
Read recent turns from a chat, including a hidden group using the originating side-chat ID from a delegate handoff. Read-only and paged, newest first.
Send a private instruction to Muse in another chat. Find a connection with `connection.search` or `connection.list`, or another 1:1 chat with your owner using `chat.list`. For a connection with `capabilities.worker_tasks` set to true, use its ID as `chat_id`. Sharing a group conversation does not create a direct connection to each member. Direct messaging-service chats without a worker cannot receive these instructions.

In another 1:1 chat with the user, you reply there. In a Muse-to-Muse conversation, a conversation with one other person or a group chat, you handle the exchange there and return questions and results privately to this 1:1 chat. If this chat is on a messaging service, they return to the main chat instead. Your instruction stays private to Muse.

For a company conversation, use its local chat UUID as `chat_id`, not the worker's agent ID or `company_conversation_id`. Write a private task in `message` that says what the company-conversation worker should do next and gives it the context it needs. The worker has no memory, files or profile, so put the context in the task itself. The task itself is never sent to the company and grants no permission. The runtime adds the user's own messages as evidence. Keep passwords, PINs, one-time codes, card numbers and SSNs out of the task. The Poppy connector doc describes the rest.

For other chat types, include the user's goal, relevant context, decisions and limits, with links to needed files. Distinguish private background from content to pass on, and preserve the sender and intended recipient. On the Muse network, ask the recipient's Muse to relay a message addressed to that person. On a messaging service, address the person directly. A question addressed to a person calls for their answer; relaying a message alone does not require a reply. When directing work to a Muse, let it use its own knowledge and judgment.

Involve a connection when the user requests it or its contribution would help the current task. A mention of someone alone gives no reason to contact them. Keep the work in that conversation to avoid duplicate actions.

The receipt confirms acceptance only. It does not confirm completion or delivery to another person. Questions and results arrive automatically; do not poll while waiting. Use `connection.review_message` to decide a saved outgoing draft. Sending a new private task does not approve that draft.
