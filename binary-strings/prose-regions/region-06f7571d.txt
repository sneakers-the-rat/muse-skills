Allowed movement families are not priority ordered:

- `humanoid`: movement is organized around an upright bipedal or person-like
  body, with a head or forward-facing focal area, an upper body, and upper limbs
  or limb ends that can support person-like actions. This includes compatible
  anthropomorphic animals, robots, and objects. Hands or lower-body anatomy may
  be outside a diagnostic crop when the subject's conventional humanoid body
  plan is otherwise unambiguous.
- `dexterous_animal`: a clearly non-humanoid animal whose natural grasping
  forelimbs can manipulate a small object while it retains a nonhuman posture,
  such as a monkey, raccoon, or otter.
- `quadruped`: an animal or creature whose ground movement and support are
  organized around four weight-bearing limbs, such as a dog, cat, cow, or
  horse, without wings as a primary movement feature.
- `avian`: a bird-like animal whose movement is organized around a beak,
  feathered wings that serve as its forelimbs, and two legs.
- `winged_animal`: a non-avian animal whose movement is organized around
  prominent wings, such as a bat, winged insect, griffin, or winged dragon.
  Existing legs remain supporting anatomy; wing motion is the safest readable
  choice.
- `serpentine`: a limbless or primarily snake-like animal or creature whose
  motion is organized around a continuous body curve, such as a snake, eel,
  worm, or serpentine dragon.
- `other_articulated`: a clearly articulated animal or creature with existing
  joints, appendages, or body segments that does not safely fit a specialized
  family. This includes genuinely mixed action-bearing body plans and unusual
  creatures. Use this instead of guessing a more specific anatomy.
- `form_agnostic`: a subject for which none of the anatomy-specific action
  families is safe. This includes a non-articulated plant or object, blob,
  flame, crystal, cloud, light, elemental, or abstract form, plus a
  non-humanoid machine, vehicle, or functional object even when it has moving
  mechanical joints. Do not use this merely because an animal or creature is
  unusual or partially outside the frame.

Apply this evidence hierarchy:

1. Use the dominant visible support and movement topology of the whole subject.
   Directly visible conflicting anatomy overrides an assumption from a face,
   recognizable identity, accessory, or isolated appendage.
2. Cropping and occlusion mean a feature is unobserved, not absent. When strong,
   unambiguous identity or species cues establish a conventional body plan and
   every visible feature is compatible with it, infer that family even when its
   diagnostic limbs are outside the frame. Apply this equally to recognizable
   humans, animals, and other conventional subjects. Do not infer unusual or
   contradictory anatomy.
3. Treat appendages by the role they play in the depicted body plan. Additional
   wings do not replace an otherwise upright bipedal plan with separate upper
   limbs; wings that are the primary forelimbs or dominant movement feature do.
4. For a mixed or unfamiliar but clearly articulated animal or creature, select
   `other_articulated` unless one specialized family safely describes the whole
   action-bearing body plan. Head or facial morphology alone does not make an
   otherwise coherent body plan mixed. A merely face-like feature does not
   establish `humanoid`; a clearly human portrait may establish it under rule 2,
   but a human-looking face never overrides visible non-humanoid anatomy.
5. Classify depicted anatomy regardless of image medium. An inert statue,
   non-mechanical toy, or drawing that clearly depicts a person, animal, or
   creature keeps that depicted body-plan family instead of becoming
   `form_agnostic` merely because it is made of stone, plastic, or pixels. A
   compatible anthropomorphic robot or object can be `humanoid`. Otherwise, a
   functional machine, vehicle, or mechanical object uses `form_agnostic`
   rather than an animal family, even when it is animal-shaped or has joints.
6. Ignore clothing, pose, camera crop, background, and held props unless they
   reveal how the subject is physically supported or can move.

Allowed confidence values are `high`, `medium`, and `low`:

- `high`: the family is established by visible topology or by an unambiguous
  conventional identity with no conflicting visible anatomy.
- `medium`: the image is partial or stylized, but one family remains clearly
  safer and better supported than the alternatives.
- `low`: the evidence cannot reliably distinguish a movement family. Return the
  best-supported raw family; the caller retains that assessment and applies a
  topology-neutral safe plan. Do not use `low` solely because diagnostic anatomy
  is outside a crop when the conventional body plan is otherwise unambiguous.
