The sources audit. Below is the source audit of
your draft file: the addresses your material stated that your units
never used, and any address in your units that your material never
stated. Beside it is the date audit: how old each address you cited
was on the reading day.

Writers may not mint addresses: an address your material never
stated is a fabrication whatever it points at, and it cannot stay.
An address is a URL; a machine identifier (a message id, an event
id, a thread id, a UUID, a database key) is not an address and
never prints, not in the body and not in the echo. A unit that
ships with zero links while its material carried real addresses is
a defect, not a style choice, and each unit's sources field is the
paper's audit: it echoes exactly the addresses its body actually
uses. Material that stated only identifiers makes an honest no-link
unit with an empty sources field. Repair with feed.update_draft:
use the zero-based unit_index and complete new values for body_md,
sources or time_sensitivity, combining each unit's changes in one call.
link the natural words the claim lives on, drop the minted address
or identifier, fix the echo. An unused address is legal when the
body makes no claim that needs it; judge each line of the audit,
not the audit's existence.

Each unit's sources field is ordered as well as audited. The
address the unit chiefly stands on goes first and the rest follow
behind it, because the paper reads that first entry as the source
you actually wrote from: a list opening on an incidental link
misreports where the unit came from. Set that order here, in the
same pass that fixes the echo.

Dates are the second half of this audit, and the bars are YOUR
story's, not one number for the whole paper. Your draft carries
`time_sensitivity`, and the measurements above are computed against
it. Each rung carries its own preference and its own replace bar, and
the audit lines above print the ones your story is actually being
judged against, so read those rather than carrying a number in your
head: a story you called `recent_3d` is current for days, while one
you called `recent_90d` or `recent_180d` is current far longer because
its material does not expire. Set that field honestly before
reading these lines, because it is what decides whether a source the
audit names is actually late.

Inside your story's band a source is current and needs nothing from
you. Past it the age is a fact the unit has to answer for, and at the
replace bar it is a source to swap wherever your material holds a
fresher address for the same claim: swap it, and where the audit
shows two addresses for one story keep the one published later.

A source whose page states NO date is neither current nor old, and it
is not a source to replace. Its `published_at` files as null and the
body carries that honestly: say the page states no publication date
rather than dating it, and never let the absence become today. Pages
with no news cycle are the ones that arrive this way, so this is
frequently the best address the claim has rather than the weakest.

When your material holds no fresher address, the claim keeps the
source it has and the unit answers for the age instead: the date
named plainly in the body, the tense past, and the sentence that
says why this still reaches the reader today. Never strip a citation
you cannot replace. A claim standing on its real address with its
date on the surface beats the same claim standing on nothing, and a
unit that ships with zero links while its material carried real
addresses is the defect this stage exists to catch.

The bar has two exemptions and neither is your own sense that a story
is still interesting.

The first is the reader's: when their own feed prompt asks for a
stretch of time that reaches back there, an address inside that
stretch is on-prompt and stays, whatever its age. The exemption is to
the bar alone. The date still rides the surface, named plainly with
the tense past, exactly as it does for any older source: the reader
asked for the window, not for a card that hides which day inside it
the thing happened.

The second is the SUBJECT'S, and it changes the copy rather than
excusing it. Some pages answer a question that does not expire: a
submissions deadline still weeks away, a schedule published ahead of
its season, a standing advisory, a guide. Reporting reaches these
deliberately, so an old date on one is not a stale story and the past
tense would misreport it. Keep the address, name the date plainly as
ever, and write what is still TRUE rather than why an old thing still
reaches the reader. A deadline that has not passed is open, not
historic. What you may never do is let the exemption cover a page
that simply went quiet: the test is whether the page's own answer has
an expiry, not whether the subject feels timeless.

A source the audit calls undated is a different problem from an old
one. Go back to what your reading served: when the page stated a
date, put it in that source's `published_at`; when it genuinely
stated none, leave it null and say so in the body if the age of the
claim matters. Never fill the field with the reading day to clear
the line.

A further section may follow those, naming pages this feed has
already published from that your draft now stands on, with the earlier
unit's own text quoted under each line. It is not a minted address and
there is nothing dishonest about it: the page is real and your story
may genuinely rest on it. It is a question, and the quoted text is how
you answer it.

Read your unit against that text and decide whether yours moves the
story SIGNIFICANTLY past it: a fact, a number, a decision, or a
consequence the reader did not already get. A fresh angle on the same
facts is not movement, and neither is a rewrite that lands the same
point in different words. If it does move, the reuse is a follow-up and
the draft stands: make sure the body says plainly what is new, so the
reader sees the advance rather than the repetition. If it does not, you
are about to print the same story twice — drop that page and stand the
unit on what the rest of your reading carries, or cut it back to the
one thing that genuinely moved. Where nothing is left after that, the
unit is the reprint, not just the page.

Judge each line on its own. A story that genuinely moved days after the
paper first ran it is a follow-up worth printing, and this section is
advice you weigh rather than a verdict already reached. Never repair
this by deleting a source the body actually uses: an unlinked reprint
is still a reprint, and now an unsourced one.

When the real misses are repaired, end your message. One stage
remains, so do not call feed.finish_step yet.
