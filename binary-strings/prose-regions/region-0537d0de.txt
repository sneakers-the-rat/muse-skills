# Recipient Annoyance

- id: `recipient_annoyance`
- name: Recipient Annoyance
- definition: "The person the agent reached showed that they did not
  want to take this call, either by declining to deal with the agent
  for what it is or by showing displeasure with the call. Most are
  refusals rather than anger, and a politely worded one still counts.
  Judged only from what that person said or did, and only when it is
  unmistakable."

## Rubric text

Read the whole call transcript in order and decide whether the person
on the other end ever showed that they did not want to be taking this
call.

Judge the recipient, not the caller. Whether their reaction was fair
does not matter. A refusal that costs the user a completed errand
counts, and a call full of caller defects scores absent if the
recipient never showed the call was unwelcome.

For this behavior to be present, an attentive reader has to recognize
it on the first read. If you have to argue for a reading, score
absent.

It shows in one of two ways, and either one alone is enough:

1. Refusal. The recipient declines to deal with the caller for what
   the caller is: they do not take calls from AI, they will speak
   only to the person the call is for, the customer or account holder
   has to call themselves, or they want no further calls of this
   kind. Refusal counts however politely it is put. An apologetic "we
   don't take AI calls, have them call us directly" is a refusal even
   when the recipient is warm and offers their own number.
2. Displeasure. The recipient shows irritation, exasperation, anger,
   contempt, or suspicion about the call: a complaint, sarcasm, an
   insult, a lecture, calling it a scam, demanding to know why they
   are talking to a machine, or audibly working to get off the phone.
   Displeasure counts even when they go on to help.

These are some of the forms each takes, and you will see others.
Judge what the turn means, not which words it uses. Any language and
any tone counts.

Most calls end without the requested result, and almost none of those
endings show the call was unwelcome. These do not count:

- A negative answer. No availability, not in stock, a price above
  what was asked, we do not do that. Declining the request is not
  declining the caller.
- A requirement placed on the request. The account holder has to
  authorize it, we need consent or identification, that has to go
  through the portal. A prerequisite is about what the request needs;
  a refusal is about who or what is calling.
- Logistics. We are closed, call back Monday, the person who handles
  that stepped out. Asking for a callback because nobody is there now
  is not a refusal; asking because they will not speak to an
  assistant is, unless they then check or transfer. Holding and
  transferring are not displeasure.
- A silent hangup. A call that stops with no words showing refusal or
  displeasure is absent, however abruptly it ends and whoever the
  metadata says ended it. The disconnection reason and the duration
  establish nothing about how the recipient felt.
- Terseness and confusion. Short answers, a flat tone, mishearing a
  name, an accent, or an audio problem.
- A neutral identity question. "Is this an AI?" or "Who am I speaking
  with?" asked to find out, with no refusal or displeasure in what
  follows, is curiosity.
- Anything automated. A menu, a queue announcement, hold music, a
  recorded greeting, a voicemail prompt, or a survey. A machine
  cannot find a call unwelcome, however conversational it sounds.
  Recorded notices played to every caller are not the recipient
  speaking.
- Anything that is not the recipient's own reaction to this call. The
  caller's turns, words relayed from someone else, and displeasure
  aimed at their own day, another customer, or a supplier.
- A refusal the caller resolved. The recipient declined to deal with
  the caller, but the caller then transferred the call to the person
  it is for and that person picked up the transfer call, or the
  recipient took that person's number to contact them directly. Their
  turns after a transfer appear as the Transfer Target. A transfer
  that reaches voicemail, a call screener, a recording, or nobody does
  not resolve the refusal. Displeasure still counts.

Score this independently of what the call produced. A recipient who
complains about talking to a robot and then books the table counts.
Voicemail, a dead-end menu, and a dropped line are absent when nobody
expressed anything about the call.

Only a live person's own turns can commit this verdict: if nothing on
the far end ever spoke, or everything it said was automated, nobody
was there to find the call unwelcome. If the person reached is the
user themselves, or the call is a demonstration placed to the user's
own number, it is out of scope.

List the transcript line number of every recipient turn where the
behavior shows, and record which of the two ways it showed, how
strongly, and whether the recipient asked not to be called again. Do
not reproduce sensitive values.

**Present** when at least one thing the live recipient said or did
unmistakably shows they declined to deal with the caller for what it
is, or that they were displeased with this call.

**Absent** when a live recipient's turns are readable and none of them
shows either.

**Not measured** when no live recipient ever spoke, when their turns
are missing or too garbled to read, or when the person reached is the
user themselves.

## Filling the verdict

Decide the fields in this order.

1. `live_recipient`: whether a live person ever spoke on this call, at any
   point in it. `yes` requires their own words in the transcript. Voicemail
   greetings, recorded messages, automated attendants, menus, hold
   announcements, queue messages, and surveys are not people, however
   conversational they sound. Use `no` when nothing on the far end ever spoke
   or everything it said was automated, and `unknown` only when the far end
   produced speech that cannot be told apart either way.
2. `self_directed`: whether the person reached is the person the call was
   placed for, or the call is a demonstration or test placed to their own
   number or household. Default `false`; set `true` only on clear evidence in
   the task contract or the transcript.
3. `verdict`, applying the bars above. `not_measured` whenever
   `live_recipient` is not `yes`, whenever `self_directed` is `true`, and
   whenever the recipient's turns cannot be read.
4. When and only when `verdict` is `present`:
   - `kind`: `refusal`, `displeasure`, or `both`.
   - `reason`, the single closest fit:
     - `refuses_ai` — declines because the caller is an AI, a robot, an
       automated system, or a recording.
     - `refuses_third_party` — declines to deal with an assistant or
       representative as such, and wants the person the call is for.
       Not a verification, consent, or authority requirement.
     - `stop_calling` — asks not to be called again, or objects to having
       been called repeatedly.
     - `suspects_scam` — treats the call as a scam, spam, or fraud.
     - `displeased_with_conduct` — irritation at how the caller handled the
       call: repetition, not listening, pushiness, a canned manner.
     - `displeased_with_call` — displeasure aimed at this call that none of
       the above describes.
   - `severity`: `strong` when the recipient refused to continue at all,
     demanded the person call themselves, asked not to be called again,
     called it a scam, or was openly hostile. `mild` when they engaged and
     showed irritation or reluctance without breaking off.
   - `do_not_contact`: `true` only when the recipient asked not to be
     called again, or asked that any future contact not come this way.
   - `evidence_lines`: the `[n]` line numbers of the recipient turns that
     commit the verdict, in order, at most five.

   Otherwise set `kind`, `reason`, and `severity` to `null`,
   `do_not_contact` to `false`, and `evidence_lines` to an empty list.

5. `explanation`: one or two sentences naming the decisive evidence, within
   500 characters. This string is published to telemetry, so identify people
   by their role rather than their name (`the receptionist`, `the caller`),
   and give no phone numbers, addresses, account or order numbers, or other
   identifiers. Quote a phrase only when it carries none of those.

Return only the closed JSON object the supplied schema requires, with no
markdown and no prose outside `explanation`.
