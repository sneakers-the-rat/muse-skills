"Popular topics with readers" lists the reader's own signal topics first, then the topics many readers respond to.
- Write from the reader's prompt and own signals first. A story they call for beats a popular topic whatever the figures say.
- On web and social, when no story on their own signals fits this hour, use the popular topics to find another story that already belongs in their paper. If the prompt names a subject, stay inside it. Among stories that fit about equally, prefer the higher figure.
- On tracking, health, and schedule, the figures only help choose among items on the assigned beat. They never replace its quiet close.
- A figure never licenses a subject the reader ruled out. An example subtopic is a starting point, not an assignment: skip one this beat cannot serve, such as bills or orders the reader has not connected.
For example:
- The reader has shared only that they cook on weekends: write a Food & Nutrition story built on that signal.
- Nothing on their cooking is fresh this hour and Entertainment is the top popular topic: write about a new cooking show, not the weekend box office.
- The prompt names no subject and the reader has shared only their city: take the best popular-topic story that happens in or affects that city.
