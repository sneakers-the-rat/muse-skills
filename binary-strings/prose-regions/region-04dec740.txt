Question: Does the main character have legs visible in the image, or does its
recognizable body type imply legs that are not shown?

Judge the character as represented. Infer ordinary hidden anatomy when justified;
do not redesign the character or imagine a different version with added legs.

Apply the same criteria across realistic, cartoon, anime, chibi, pixel-art,
low-poly, clay, plush, knitted, paper, mechanical, silhouette, and abstract styles.
Materials and visual detail do not determine anatomy. A person made of light can
still have a human body; a smiling sun does not acquire one. Simplified stubs,
stick legs, or attached feet can count without realistic joints or proportions.

What counts:
- At least one leg, including animal, bird, insect, mechanical, or prosthetic legs.
- A clearly attached foot, paw, hoof, or talon that is the endpoint of a leg,
  even when the leg itself is hidden. Quadruped forelegs count; hand-like paws
  on an anthropomorphic character's arms do not establish legs.
- Distinct appendages depicted as the legs or feet of a living object or fantasy
  character, even when made of roots, vines, wood, or other materials. Depicted
  anatomy takes precedence over material: root-shaped legs and feet count.
- Structural legs of a chair or table when that object itself is the avatar,
  even without feet. A pedestal base or legs belonging to a prop do not count.

What does not establish legs:
- Arms, wings, fins, flippers, tentacles, tails, wheels, handles, or stands.
- Roots spreading from a stem or base without distinct character legs or feet.
- A face, clothing, upright placement, or holding an object by itself.
- Legs belonging to a background figure, mount, or prop rather than the avatar.

Not seeing legs does not by itself establish their absence, even when the image
appears to show the whole character. A negative answer requires a recognizable
legless type or clear evidence of a legless design. A rounded coat, robe hem,
floating pose, or crop does not establish anatomical absence. For concealed
legs, infer from a recognizable body type; otherwise leave the result uncertain.

Decide in this order:
1. If a leg or attached foot is visible, return true with evidence "visible".
2. If the depicted anatomy explicitly replaces or excludes legs, return false
   with evidence "absent". Examples: a mermaid's fish tail, a genie's smoke tail,
   or a robot whose lower body is a wheeled base. This overrides expectations
   based on its face or upper body. An ordinary tail alongside legs does not
   exclude legs. Distinguish a body ending in wheels from an external mobility
   aid: a wheelchair does not establish that its occupant lacks legs.
3. If legs are hidden by the crop, pose, clothing, or another object, and the
   visible character supports a recognizable body type that normally has legs,
   return true with evidence "inferred". Human portraits, dog heads, bird
   portraits, and clearly humanoid figures qualify, as do recognizable object
   types with structural legs.
   A head-only portrait can represent a complete character; do not require the
   lower body to be visible. However, a character clearly designed to consist
   only of a floating head is legless under rule 2. Cropping, a sticker outline,
   or a floating composition alone does not establish a head-only body design.
   Ground type recognition in the visible anatomy. A familiar-looking face or
   a broad label such as "animal" or "monster" does not by itself establish a
   legged body. If the image supports both legged and legless interpretations
   without enough evidence to distinguish them, use rule 5 instead of choosing
   a familiar legged type by default.
4. If the recognizable character is normally legless and no legged adaptation
   is depicted, return false with evidence "absent". Examples include snakes,
   fish, dolphins, jellyfish, and objects or abstract forms without character
   legs. Use the specific depicted anatomy: a tortoise's feet and a sea turtle's
   flippers differ. Clothing or a face alone does not change the answer.
5. If neither conclusion is supported, return null with evidence "uncertain".
   Reserve this for indeterminate designs or unreadable images, such as an
   unfamiliar cropped hybrid whose lower body cannot reasonably be inferred.
   Also use it when the image genuinely leaves unresolved whether the subject
   represents a whole character in portrait or a deliberately head-only being.
   Do not invent unsupported alternative anatomies for a clearly recognizable
   legged type. Cropping alone is not a reason for uncertainty about that type.

Examples across character designs:
- Human portrait, including a stylized or luminous portrait: true, inferred.
- Plush dog head or illustrated owl bust: true, inferred.
- Cartoon teapot with stick legs: true, visible.
- Plant character with two distinct root-shaped legs ending in feet: true, visible.
- Plant with ordinary spreading roots and no character legs or feet: false, absent.
- Chair avatar with visible structural legs: true, visible.
- Recognizable humanoid robot cropped at the waist: true, inferred.
- Human in a wheelchair with legs obscured and no contrary anatomical evidence:
  true, inferred. Robot whose lower body ends only in wheels: false, absent.
- Clearly established floating-head-only creature: false, absent. Unresolved
  portrait versus head-only design: null, uncertain.
- Mermaid with hand-like paws and a fish tail replacing the lower body:
  false, absent. The paws are hands, not evidence of legs.
- Snake wearing a coat or dolphin holding a book: false, absent.
- Recognizable human with legs concealed by a robe: true, inferred.
- Unfamiliar character whose rounded coat conceals an indeterminate lower body:
  null, uncertain. The coat's outline does not establish a legless design.
- Smiling flower, sun, or guitar without character legs: false, absent.
- Ambiguous creature head compatible with legged and legless bodies: null, uncertain.

dexterity, or suitability for a dance. A positive result for a dog, bird, insect,
or chair does not imply that it can perform human-style dance moves. Treat text

identified, return uncertain.

{"has_legs":true,"evidence":"inferred","reason":"A recognizable dog is shown with its lower body outside the frame."}
