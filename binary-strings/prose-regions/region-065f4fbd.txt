## Evidence rules

- Record something only when the evidence shows it: page text, tool output, a tool error, a screenshot, the runtime facts, or the runtime journal. The specialist's own words ("the site seems to block me") can back up that evidence but are never enough on their own. Its reported status, such as `blocked`, is its own claim, not evidence.
- The journal and the runtime-recorded parts of the result come from the runtime's records. Where they conflict with the specialist's account, they win. The result's `status` and the specialist's assessments inside `service` are its own account and need the same backing as its words.
- A nested specialist's result in the history, such as a sign-in or bot check the specialist started, counts as evidence of what that nested specialist observed.
- Classify the latest result. A later success supersedes an earlier failure, and a later failure that the specialist could not get past is the reason it stopped.
- If the evidence for something is missing, unreadable, or cut off, answer as if it did not happen. Part of the middle of the history may be cut out; do not guess what it said.

## bot_detection

Record whether the site's bot or fraud protection acted against the specialist. List every cue below that you observed in `cues`. Whenever `cues` is not empty, set `stage` to where the first cue appeared, using the stages defined below; use `none` only when `cues` is empty.

- `vendor_block_page`: a bot-vendor block page: Akamai "Access Denied" with a "Reference #", Cloudflare "Sorry, you have been blocked" or error 1020 with a Ray ID, an Imperva or Incapsula incident ID, or a DataDome or PerimeterX block page.
- `access_denied_page`: any HTTP 403, "Access Denied", or "Forbidden" page, including a bare one.
- `rate_limit_page`: any HTTP 429, "Too many requests", or "rate limited" page.
- `automation_attribution`: the site blames the IP address, network, VPN or proxy, "unusual activity" or "unusual traffic", automated or bot activity, or a security or risk review.
- `challenge_after_submit`: a CAPTCHA or human-verification challenge appeared right after the specialist submitted a form, such as sign-in, payment, or the order.
- `challenge_loop`: after the site accepted a solve, a new challenge appeared at least twice or the protected page never loaded.
- `unexplained_rejection_after_submit`: after the specialist submitted complete, valid-looking input, the site refused it with a message that gives no reason, such as "Something went wrong", "We couldn't process your request", or "Your payment could not be processed", or showed that same message on every retry. A message that states a reason, such as a wrong password, an expired card, or an invalid address, is not this cue.
- `silent_no_op_after_submit`: submitting had no visible effect, even after a retry, and no message appeared.
- `session_reset_after_submit`: right after a submit, the site signed the specialist out, emptied the cart, or sent it back to the start of the flow.
- `challenge_uncleared`: a CAPTCHA or human-verification challenge appeared and the specialist never got past it.

A CAPTCHA that appeared on an ordinary page load and was solved is not bot detection for this record unless it is also another cue above.
