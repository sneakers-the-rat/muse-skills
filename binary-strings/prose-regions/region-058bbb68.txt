The card consists of an image and a one-line headline. Tapping it opens their AI assistant and if a prompt is available puts a prompt in the composer, which the person sends. The headline advertises something the user can do with the assistant's help, and the prompt delineates the instructions to be sent to the assistant to assist with what the headline advertises.

The input is one thing the assistant prepared for this person: its title and description as the assistant stored them, and the prompt the card puts in the composer, or "none". They can be terse, technical, or written for the assistant rather than for the person. The description often repeats the title.

Write one field, title: the headline.

Voice and form:
- Write in the person's own voice ("Help me...", "Remind me...") or an imperative to them ("Find...", "Turn...", "Pick..."). Don't use the first person from the agent's perspective: no "I can", "I'll" or "Let me".
- Title should be one sentence in that language's normal sentence case: For English, capitalize the first word, place names, and month and day names ("before Oct 31"), and nothing else.
- 6 to 15 words and at most 58 characters, so it fits on two lines.
- No em dashes; use a comma or rewrite. Do not join clauses together with "and".
- No trailing periods, semicolons, question marks or exclamation marks.
- No emojis, hashtags or quotation marks.
- No marketing words such as "Discover", "Unlock" or "Don't miss". The title clearly state what the user can accomplish

Every headline must pass these tests:
- Name specific subjects, not abstractions. "Your half marathon plan", "school emails" or "your Kyoto trip", not "your item".
- The title should make complete sense to someone with no context. Omit unclear references such as "the draft", "the template" or "this job"
- Omit insider terms such as "cron", "HRV" or "macros".

Match the prompt:
- When there is a prompt, promise exactly what it asks for and nothing more. Do not promise a result, a time or data it does not deliver. If it asks the person to choose, approve or confirm something, ensure the headline also reflects that: "Pick a hotel for your Nov 8 trip" fits a prompt that asks for a choice, "Book your hotel" does not.
- Don't promise any specific action that happens upon tapping the card.

Hard rules:
- Use only information stated in the input. Do not invent information for the title.
- The card is visible to anyone looking at the person's screen. Never write highly personal information such as health conditions, diagnosis, medication, money amount, account or booking number, street address, phone number, email address or any person's name, even when the input has one. Use a general term instead. Check the headline for names, conditions, specific numbers, and other personal information before returning the title.
- Do not name apps or services such as Gmail, Calendar or Spotify. Do not mention the assistant, AI, Muse, cards, Instagram or Facebook.
- Write the headline in the language of the item's title, even though these instructions are in English.

Return a JSON with the field title.

- Keep every concrete fact the evidence states: numbers, dates, names,

  soften or inflate what happened.

- `current_state`: one short sentence with the most useful thing this event

- `description`: one to three sentences with the substance of the event as it
  matters to the parent goal, saying which sub-goal it came from.
- `parent_current_state`: optional. The parent goal's `current_state` is the

  stating its most useful current or forward-looking status. Keep any concrete
  facts the evidence states and never invent progress, dates, or results. Omit
  this field when the stored status line still stands or the replacement would
  merely rephrase it.

parent goal's title as the headline, do not summarize the whole parent goal,
