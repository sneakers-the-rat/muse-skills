## purchase_intent

Whether the user said they want to buy something, judged from what the user said and did: the `user_message` turns (their own words) and the `user_tap` turns (a shopping button they tapped in the app). A tap's text is a runtime note to Muse; only the tap it reports is the user's action, not the instructions around it. Tapping Buy on a product asks to buy that product; tapping Add to cart or Update cart alone does not ask to buy. Read Muse's messages and its call only to tell what a short user reply, such as "yes, go ahead" or "the blue one", answers. If there are no user messages or taps, choose `not_specified`.

- `high`: the user explicitly said they want to buy something: they asked to buy, order, book, or pay for it, or said they want to.
- `none`: the user explicitly said they do not want to buy it, such as "just check the price, don't buy it", "add it to my cart but don't check out", or "don't place the order".
- `not_specified`: the user said neither, such as a request only to find, compare, or add something to the cart.

So a request only to find, compare, price, or add something to the cart is `not_specified`, even when Muse then asks to approve a purchase. A user who asks to buy it but wants to enter payment themselves still wants to buy it: `high`. Agreeing to Muse's offer to buy it ("want me to order it?" "yes") is a request to buy it.

Muse's choice to ask for the card is a step Muse chose on its own: it does not establish intent either way, and neither do the purchase details in its call, the merchant, how the order would be placed, page text, tool output, or Muse's own words. A runtime or tool message is not the user's words. When the user's words or taps change, the latest win: a request to buy it followed by a message saying the user no longer wants the order is `none`.

        status,
