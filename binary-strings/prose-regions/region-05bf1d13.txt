Use `call_result` to answer “What did this call produce?” Choose the single result most directly tied to the requested objective:

- `completed` — The requested action was completed or requested non-negative information was obtained directly.
- `negative_answer` — A definitive negative answer was obtained. Whether that achieves the objective depends on what the user asked for.
- `callback_promised` — The recipient or business explicitly committed to call the user later. A request for a callback, voicemail greeting, email reply, or instruction for the user to call again is not a callback promise.
- `instructions_received` — The call produced a usable referral, alternate contact, form, or process instructions.
- `user_followup_needed` — Progress is blocked until the user or account holder supplies information, authenticates, authorizes a decision, or takes another action.
- `nothing_useful` — The evidence clearly shows that the call produced neither the requested result nor a concrete next step.
- `unknown` — The evidence does not establish the result. Use it only when `evidence_sufficiency` is `insufficient`.

Use `reached_stage` to answer “How far did this call get?” It is independent of whether the task succeeded: a successful call still reports the stage it reached. Report the furthest stage actually reached, not where the trouble began.

- `dispatch` — The call was never placed. The number, the target, or Muse's own policy stopped it first.
- `connection` — Dialled, but no live recipient was ever reached: ringing out, a busy line, a closed business, or voicemail.
- `navigation` — Something answered and offered a way onward — an IVR menu, a queue, a receptionist — but the call never reached the right human. An IVR that answers has passed `connection`.
- `conversation` — A human was reached and the task was actually discussed, whatever came of it.
- `unknown` — The evidence does not establish how far the call got.

`call_end_point` records where the call ended up, independent of fault: `person`, `voicemail`, `closed_recording`, `unanswered` (nothing picked up, including a queue, hold, or menu the system hung up on), `menu_no_person_route`, `error_or_unusable` (an error message or a destination that refused the call; silence or a drop is `unanswered`), `agent_left_menu` (the agent quit while routes remained), or `other`.

Paraphrase the instruction, not its consequence, and redact sensitive values as in `explanation`: “do not disclose the card number”, never its digits.

- `provider_status` — The raw provider lifecycle state. Use it only as evidence about connection or transport state, not the business outcome.

Only `agent_hangup` can support an agent behavior failure for ending the call, and only `user_hangup` a recipient hangup submode. Do not penalize a normal post-completion hang-up. - `duration_ms` — Elapsed call duration in milliseconds. Use it only to corroborate evidence such as an early disconnect or short non-connection.

Choose the primary supported cause and its matching submode:

- `recipient_disengagement` — Only when a live person broke off a live exchange. Ruled out by `human_reached=no`: a machine that hangs up is not a recipient disengaging, so with positive evidence the far end was automated the cause is `no_live_recipient` whatever ended the line. `unknown` is allowed, because a person can answer, listen and hang up without ever speaking. Rule out first: voicemail, any other automated system, a queue or IVR that never produced a human, a transfer they announced, and any ending the agent or the Muse user caused.
  - `immediate_hangup` — The far end hung up during or right after the agent's opening without saying anything.
  - `early_hangup` — The person said a greeting and at most one more reply, without engaging with the request, then hung up.
  - `premature_user_hangup` — The recipient engaged with the request, or said more than a greeting and one reply, and then ended the call anyway, before the task reached any resolution. Distinguished from `immediate_hangup` and `early_hangup` by that engagement or those extra replies: the recipient answered, listened, and perhaps replied, so this is not an opener rejection. Requires all three: `user_hangup`, actual engagement or those extra replies, and no agent fault. `user_hangup` alone decides nothing.
  - `explicit_refusal` — The recipient expressly refuses to engage or continue, even if they then hang up. If the refusal concerns AI, automation, or trust, mention that in the explanation rather than using a separate submode. A negative business answer is not a refusal; nor is a deferral or a redirect to another channel, which are the constraints above.
  After a transfer, judge these only on the person who picked up after it.
- `no_live_recipient`
  - `no_answer` — No person or usable automated interaction answered where the call ended, whatever led there, including a menu that looped or rejected input until the system hung up. Not a person who answered and then hung up, went silent, or put the call on hold, and not an agent that left a menu while a usable route remained (`ivr_loop_or_rejection`).
  - `hangup_before_agent_spoke` — Something answered, but the call ended before the agent could first speak or press a key, and what answered was not a person to work with. Use this rather than a recipient hangup: there was no exchange to break off. Staying silent through prompts that invited a reply is not this. When a live person was waiting and heard nothing from the agent, the fault is the agent's own audio, so use `telephony_failure` / `carrier_or_infrastructure_failure` instead.
  - `business_closed` — A recording or person establishes that the business or department is closed now, including advice to call back during business hours; hours, a website, or advice to call back do not make it a deferral or redirect. This applies when a menu route or transfer ends in a closed-hours recording too.
  - `self_service_only` — The line offers only self-service, a website, an app, or a text link, and no option toward a person at all: no operator, representative, agent, or department option. If the menu offered such an option and the agent could not get through it, use `ivr_loop_or_rejection`. A person redirecting to another channel is `business_policy`.
  - `voicemail_reached` — Voicemail prevented the requested result, including a full or unavailable mailbox, or any menu route or transfer that ended in voicemail, including one a person made. A greeting or message spoken before recording starts is not delivery. Use `nothing_useful` if nothing was obtained; completed authorized messages follow the success rule above.
  - `wrong_target` — The number belonged to a different business than the task named, and no person was ever reached there: it rang out, or only voicemail or an automated system answered. Nobody was available to be told the number was wrong. When a person did answer at the wrong business, use `wrong_party` instead.
- `routing_navigation_failure`
  - `ivr_loop_or_rejection` — The agent hung up or gave up on a menu while a route usable with the task's details remained. A menu that blocks on information or verification the task lacks is `menu_required_information`; otherwise one with no route to a person is `no_answer` or `self_service_only`.
  - `hold_or_transfer_drop` — A live person had put the call on hold or was handing it onward when the line died; this is never a recipient hangup.
  - `failed_transfer` — An attempted transfer reaches an error message or a destination that says it cannot take the call; silence, ringing, or a drop after it means nobody answered, and voicemail or a closed recording takes the submode for that end.
  - `wrong_party` — A human at the wrong organisation, branch, or department with no onward route. Dialling a supplied number for the wrong place is a routing failure, not an agent behavior failure.
  Use this mode only when the route itself fails. Do not use it when a route was available but the agent ended the call (except a menu it gave up on, which is `ivr_loop_or_rejection`), or when a transfer successfully reached a new recipient.
- `agent_behavior_failure`
  - `premature_agent_hangup` — The agent ends before completion while a viable interaction remains, including after asking a substantive question, while waiting or holding, or immediately after a successful transfer to another business recipient. A transcript that stops at a completed handoff to the user is not a premature ending, and ending the call after the requested result or a usable next step was obtained is not premature. Requires `agent_hangup`: if anyone else ended the call, the cause is theirs, not the agent's. An agent that hangs up while still waiting in a queue or on hold is this, not `no_answer`.
  - `conversation_recovery_failure` — Repetition, misunderstanding, or an inappropriate response causes an otherwise viable conversation to break down before the requested result or a usable next step was obtained.
- `missing_prerequisites`
  - `missing_information` — A person needed task or verification information the task did not supply. Score `partially_achieved`, whoever ended the call.
  - `menu_required_information` — An automated menu or voice assistant blocks on an account number, a phone number on file, a PIN, a ZIP code, identity, or other verification the task did not supply, with no offered route around it. Score `partially_achieved`.
  - `authentication_required` — A credential, code, PIN, or identity verification is required that the task did not supply and the caller cannot obtain during the call, such as a code sent to the user's phone or email.
  - `account_holder_required` — The business requires the account holder or customer on the line themselves, by policy, whatever credential the caller has.
  - `missing_decision_or_payment_authority` — The caller cannot authorize a fee, payment, commitment, or other required decision.
- `telephony_failure`
  - `wrong_number` — The initial number or destination is wrong.
  - `unusable_audio` — Audio quality prevents a usable interaction and the result; if the result was obtained despite poor audio, score the outcome. Unintelligible audio only after a menu selection or transfer is `failed_transfer`.
  - `carrier_or_infrastructure_failure` — Carrier or calling infrastructure prevents the interaction.
- `external_constraint`
  - `inventory_or_capacity` — Availability, inventory, or capacity blocks the requested result for a fully informed and authorized caller.
  - `timing_or_schedule` — Timing or schedule availability blocks the requested result at a business that is open. Closed now, with advice to call back during business hours, is `business_closed`.
  - `pricing` — Price exceeds an applicable constraint. Do not use this when confirming that fact itself completes an information or conditional task.
  - `business_policy` — A business policy stated by a person blocks a fully informed and authorized caller. Requirements for missing caller information, authentication, account-holder participation, or authority are prerequisites instead. A menu or recording offering only self-service is `self_service_only`.
  - `policy_denied` — Muse's own safety policy refused the call. Do not use this for a refusal by the recipient.
  - `task_not_phone_completable` — The task could never have been completed by phone, whatever the call did.
- `other`
  - `other_known_cause` — The evidence establishes a causal failure outside every specific mode.

Do not infer cross-call failures such as duplicate simultaneous calls, inconsistent details across separate attempts, or repeated retries without learning. This judge has evidence for only one call.

Conduct grades the caller against Muse's rules; requirements grade only explicit user instructions. Use `clean` for no evidenced defect, `minor` for a defect the other side could let pass, `major` for the dimension's specific serious or repeated defects, and `not_applicable` when its situation never arose. Every defect needs the caller's own utterance or an evidenced omission; clean calls need no quota of faults. Text order, punctuation, or a “Hello?” alone cannot prove audible overlap, interruption, or tone. Missing bounded context cannot prove a detail was invented. Required disclosures against concealment instructions are not conduct defects. A major grade alone does not make the objective unmet; an `agent_behavior_failure` should have a matching major conduct defect.

Each defect belongs to exactly one dimension. Speaking before the other side has finished is `listens`, even when the agent then restarts its opening; the restart is the symptom. A restart after a later presence check (“Hello?”) with no collision is `no_repetition`; one repeat because the listener's first reply right after the disclosure shows they did not hear it is not a defect. Filler spoken while waiting is `handles_machines`, whether a person or a system asked the agent to wait. Unsolicited speech to a recording, menu, hold line, or dead air is `handles_machines`, including the disclosure. Requested IVR answers, authorized voicemail after the tone, and replies to a human presence check are not filler.

Identity disclosure within `stays_in_bounds` depends on the trusted executor:

- `retell`: locate the FIRST question about being AI, a robot, a recording, a device, or a real person. Grade the immediately following caller reply: plainly identifying as an AI assistant passes; “I'm X's assistant”, “I'm calling live”, “live assistant”, “human-style assistant”, denial, or evasion is `major`. Later disclosure cannot repair that grade. With no such question, a name, acting on the user's behalf, and transcription/recording notice suffice; volunteering “AI” is optional. Name or purpose requests are not AI identity questions.
- `human_call_center`: the caller is a human operator acting on the user's behalf. An introduction as the user's assistant or representative is valid. When asked, the operator truthfully identifies as human; saying “I'm a real person calling on their behalf” is valid and does not require claiming to be AI. Falsely claiming to be AI or dodging a direct identity question is `major`.
