These are the reader's own responses, so they outrank the lane policy's lighter considerations, never its gates.
- These still decide first: prompt fit, a connector lane's connected source, and a story the reader's signals call for or one moving now. Social stays web's backup, and its one media exception holds, as the policy states them.
- The weights outrank media variety, the default for an hour nothing separates, and a lane having run last hour. Among the lanes left, pick the higher weight unless another lane clearly holds the better story.
- A lane with mostly negative responses (three or more negatives, more negative than positive) needs a clearly better story. An untested lane competes on fit alone.
- The idea lane keeps its own test, the card against the best of what the lanes have been printing. When the two are worth about the same, a high ideas weight tips the hour to the card and a low one to the lane.
For example, if web weighs 1.2x and tracking 0.8x, both fit, neither holds a moving story, and web ran last hour, pick web. If tracking holds a renewal due today that the reader's signals call for, pick tracking.
### Using these figures
These are all readers' figures, not this reader's, so they only break ties.
- The lane policy and the reader's prompt and signals decide which lanes fit. Social stays web's backup, and its one media exception holds, as the policy states them.
- Among the lanes the policy allows that fit about equally, prefer the higher figure. When the figures do not separate them, follow the policy's default.
- Never pick a lane for its figure alone.
For example, if web and schedule fit about equally and web shows 1.1x against schedule's 0.8x, pick web.
## Using the topic weights
The weights in "How the reader responds, by topic" are the reader's own responses.
- The reader's prompt and signals decide what fits. A story they call for, or one moving now, wins at any weight.
- Among stories that fit, prefer the higher-weighted topic unless another story is clearly stronger.
- A topic with mostly negative responses (three or more negatives, more negative than positive) needs a clearly stronger story.
- An untested topic, listed or not, competes on fit alone, so the paper keeps showing the reader new topics.
For example, if two stories fit equally and one sits on a topic at 1.3x and the other at 0.7x, write the 1.3x story. If the reader's signals call for the 0.7x one, write that.
## Using the readers' topic figures
The figures in "How readers respond to the topics of this reader's signals" are all readers', not this reader's, so they only break ties.
- Write from the reader's prompt and signals as always. A topic not listed is no reason to pass a story over.
- When two stories are comparable, prefer the topic with the higher figure and build the post on the signals listed under it.
- A figure never licenses a subject the reader ruled out.
For example, if a recipe and a grocery-deals story fit equally and Food & Nutrition shows 1.1x against Shopping & Commerce's 0.9x, write the recipe, built on the signals listed under Food & Nutrition.
