Perform a short sequence of actions on a tab of your research window, such as clicking, typing, choosing an option, scrolling, dragging or navigating.

State the intent of the actions briefly. Always start with the element's reference from the view, in `ref`. When the element has no reference, such as a picture, a map, a canvas or a slider, or an action on its reference failed, describe it in `look`: say what it is, its visible text or picture, and where it sits, for example "the Reviews tab under the product title" or "the slider handle at the left end of the track". A drag describes where it starts in `from_look` and where it ends in `to_look`, and a hold describes the control to press. Add `within` with an element's reference to search only inside it, such as a results list; a drag takes `from_within` and `to_within`.

A click, fill, hover or scroll can give `ref` and `look` together: the element, or else the element the description finds. The browser uses the reference. When it cannot find that element anymore, for example after the page redrew it, it places the description on a fresh screenshot and acts there only when the description matches exactly one place; otherwise nothing more is sent and the result shows the current view. Actions after that one in the same call are not sent, because their references came from the earlier view.

Choosing an option, ticking a box and waiting take element references. Typing takes the field's reference too; to type into a field that has no reference, or whose reference failed, describe the field in `look`, and the browser clicks it and types into it. A key press takes the element's reference, or no target to press the key wherever the focus is. A scroll takes the reference of the area to scroll inside, a `look` description of an area that has none, such as a map or a list, or no target to scroll the page. Drags take `from_look` and `to_look` descriptions, and holds take a `look` description. Include only steps whose targets are already known, and stop where the next step depends on what happens.

Most fills leave out `entry`, and the browser enters the text its usual way. `typing` presses a key for each character, for a field that reacts to each key. `paste` pastes the whole text at once, for a field that expects pasted text. With either, `replace` still puts the text in place of the field's text and `append` still adds it after. `entry` takes the field's reference alone, without a look description.

A grounding model places each look target on a screenshot taken just before the action runs. When it cannot place a target, nothing runs and the result says why: `not_visible` when the target is not on screen, so scroll or open what holds it; `ambiguous` when several things match, so describe the one you mean more precisely; `unsupported` when one gesture cannot do it, so split the step. For each placed target the result reports the gesture, how confident the placement was and why.

The result reports each action and includes the updated view with a current screenshot. When an action opens a new tab, the result names the tab and shows its view. The sequence stops early when the page changes, a target is refused, a decision is needed or an outcome is uncertain. Do not repeat an action whose outcome is uncertain.

A refused step says why. `covered` means something lies over the target, so close it or reach the target another way. `not_visible` means the target is off-screen or hidden, so scroll or open what holds it. `disabled` means the page waits for something first. `page_unresponsive` means the page did not answer in time and the action may still have run, so observe with `browser.observe` before repeating it. `page_busy` means the page was still busy and nothing was sent, so repeat the step. `site_in_use` means another browser is acting on this site right now, so wait a minute before you try again or research another source meanwhile. `needs_reference` means this browser can do that step only through the element's reference, such as scrolling inside an area on some browsers or typing text with line breaks; when the target has no reference, reach the information another way the site offers or use another source. `entry_unsupported` means this browser cannot type or paste the way `entry` asks, so fill without `entry`.

This tool cannot enter secrets, upload or download files, or save pages, and your window is signed in to nothing. When a page asks you to sign in, pay or prove you are human, or refuses you access, leave it and use another source or the web index.
