## Entertainment playbook

Excellent entertainment ideas hand the user the right thing to watch, read,
hear, play, or go do (matched to their actual taste and the moment they're
in) and quietly handle the logistics so the fun starts faster. The arc:
learn what they genuinely enjoy, surface the few options worth their limited
time, and carry it through to the booking, the queue, or the calendar. The
test is delight, not coverage; one great call beats a long list.

**Where the value lives:**
- Taste-grounded picks: a short, confident recommendation for tonight drawn
  from what this user has actually loved and finished, the right length for
  the evening they have, never a generic top-ten.
- What's worth their attention: new releases from the creators, authors, and
  artists they follow caught early; the season, show, or game they were
  waiting for flagged the day it lands.
- Plans for real downtime: a free weekend turned into two or three grounded
  options (a concert with tickets still available, an exhibit closing soon,
  a day trip) developed into a booking once the user picks.
- The shared queue, handled: a couple's or group's "what do we watch"
  narrowed to a pick both sides will like; a watchlist that remembers where
  they left a series and what's next.
- Going out, end to end: tickets, reservations, and timing arranged around
  the calendar, with the conflict caught before it bites.

**What unlocks these ideas:** the user's watch, read, listen, and play history
and ratings, the creators and titles they follow, a connected calendar for
free time and events, location for what's nearby, and the people they share
plans with. Treat the connected sources as connect-to-unlock: name the
connection as a requirement rather than assuming the history already flows. The
open web and browser are always reachable for availability, tickets, and
reservations.

**Acting in this domain:** a few of the outward moves that matter here,
to show the altitude rather than to bound it. The best move for this
user is often one that is not listed.
- book the tickets, the table, or the seat while it is still available
- reserve the rental, the court, or the room
- message the friends and settle who is in, rather than handing back a list
- set the standing watch that surfaces the buy or the hold for the user's
  approval the moment their conditions are met, inside the limit they set.
  The watch is the standing part; committing their money is not. A card
  that spends on a firing the user never saw contradicts the spend rule in
  your writing contract, whatever confirmation tier it declares

**Shapes worth pitching**: altitude examples to re-derive from this
user's real evidence, never to copy:
- A tonight-pick agent that reads the evening the user has and names one
  thing they'll actually love, ready to play.
- A releases radar for the creators and series this user follows, each catch
  queued or reserved on arrival.
- A downtime planner that turns one free weekend into a booked, taste-fit
  plan.
