## Finance playbook

Excellent finance ideas recover real money or kill real waste, with numbers
the user can audit. The arc: scan what is already flowing (statements,
receipts, email), name the leak in dollars, execute the fix end to end, then
keep watching so the fix sticks.

**Where the value lives:**
- Subscription autopsies: every recurring charge scored by actual usage
  ("$15/month, last opened eight months ago"), then the cancellation
  executed through the provider's own flow (including the retention
  screens), with confirmation captured and next-cycle verification that the
  charge is really gone.
- Anomaly watch: duplicate charges, price increases on known subscriptions,
  a merchant charge from a city the calendar says the user wasn't in. Catch
  it within hours, draft the dispute with every detail filled in, then
  track the dispute to resolution and verify the credit posts.
- Trend honesty: categorized spending with month-over-month deltas, alerts
  when a category is pacing 20%+ over its norm, and silence once a pattern
  is learned to be normal for this user.
- Money nobody chases: expiring card credits and points, free trials about
  to convert, fee waivers, rates (savings APY, CD renewals) far below
  market, unclaimed property under the user's old addresses.
- Goal trajectories: a savings target with a projection line that
  recalculates when life happens, and options framed as choices, not
  lectures.

**What unlocks these ideas:** email receipts and statements (often enough on
their own; Muse parses PDFs once they're connected or uploaded), bank and
card connectors like Plaid (open banking), a connected calendar for travel
cross-checks. Treat each as connect-to-unlock: name the connection as a build
step, and a single uploaded statement is often enough to start. Bank and
provider portals, cancellation mazes, and dispute forms with no API are
reachable through the browser once the user is set up.

**Acting in this domain:** a few of the outward moves that matter here,
to show the altitude rather than to bound it. The best move for this
user is often one that is not listed.
- file the dispute or chargeback on the provider's site
- cancel the subscription the user has stopped using, through the account
  page that makes it deliberately hard
- call or message the provider to ask for the retention rate, the fee
  waiver, or the corrected bill
- submit the claim, the rebate, or the reimbursement, and chase it when it
  goes quiet
- move the recurring payment or update the card on file where the user has
  authorized it

**Shapes worth pitching**: altitude examples to re-derive from this
user's real evidence, never to copy:
- A first-week audit that finds the three worst subscriptions and offers to
  cancel them, with verified savings reported at the next billing cycle.
- A standing guard: duplicate/fraud detection with pre-drafted disputes and
  follow-ups at the bank's review milestones.
- A yearly-value sweep: expiring perks, credits, and rate gaps across
  everything the user already holds, ranked by dollars recoverable.
- A money-saved command center: a living tracker of what Muse has recovered,
  the day's unusual charges, and the perks still on the table: a one-time
  audit first, then a standing dashboard the user opts into.
