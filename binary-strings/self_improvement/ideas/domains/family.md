## Family playbook

Excellent family ideas absorb the operational load of running a household
of people: school logistics, kids' activities, appointments, and the
thousand small deadlines that all default to one parent's head. The arc:
read the streams the family already generates, execute the routine parts,
and surface only decisions, until the user stops reading school email
entirely because nothing gets dropped.

**Where the value lives:**
- School-stream triage: every portal email classified into act / calendar /
  ignore; permission slips pre-filled for one-tap submission; spirit-week
  days on the calendar with the missing hat found, compared, and ready to
  order; supply lists turned into carts with price checks across retailers.
- Logistics resolution: early dismissals and schedule changes checked
  against both parents' calendars, a pickup plan proposed, the message to
  the other parent or backup family already drafted.
- Registration windows: camps, seasons, and activities tracked from open to
  close, last year's selections pre-staged, escalating reminders so the
  deadline never wins.
- Family health cadence: kids' physicals before tryouts, eye exams, dental
  cleanings, the pet's vaccines: due dates tracked, providers researched
  where missing, slots found that fit the family calendar.
- Captured intent: "we should do something fun with the kids this summer"
  said in passing becomes three grounded options the next morning (based
  on what this family has actually loved), developed into bookings when
  one is picked.

**What unlocks these ideas:** these lean on data the user connects: shared
family calendars, Gmail or Outlook (school portals live in email), messaging
channels, the people in the user's life. Treat each as connect-to-unlock: name
the connection as a requirement rather than assuming it already flows. Portals
without APIs are reachable through the browser once the user is set up.

**Acting in this domain:** a few of the outward moves that matter here,
to show the altitude rather than to bound it. The best move for this
user is often one that is not listed.
- message the other parent, the sitter, the coach, or the school on the
  user's behalf
- book the appointment, the class, or the sitter, and put it on the shared
  calendar
- reply to the school portal or the group thread with the answer the family
  has already agreed
- order the supplies the week actually needs
- chase the form, the permission slip, or the reimbursement that is late

**Shapes worth pitching**: altitude examples to re-derive from this
user's real evidence, never to copy:
- A school-email autopilot that turns the weekly flood into two or three
  prepared decisions.
- A family appointments radar: who is due for what, with researched
  providers and bookable slots.
- An intent catcher that develops casually mentioned family plans into
  concrete, bookable options.
