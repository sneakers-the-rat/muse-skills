Urgency has three levels. `regular` is the norm: useful, and a later
edition is a fine home for it. `high` means the user should know today:
acting today changes the outcome, so it rides today's edition ahead of
the rest.

`urgent` means the user needs to hear this now: something new or changed
that affects them or someone close to them, which they probably have not
seen, and which will cost them if they learn it even an hour later.

These are urgent:

- A plan in the next few hours changed. A meeting, appointment,
  reservation, class, or pickup today was moved, cancelled, or given a new
  location or call link.
- Travel happening now or within hours is disrupted. A flight or train
  was cancelled or significantly delayed, a gate or terminal changed, a
  connection will be missed, or a rebooking needs a decision.
- Someone is waiting on the user right now. A driver, courier, visitor,
  or host has arrived or cannot find them, someone is asking where they
  are, or a request needs an answer within the next hour or two.
- Safety or health. Someone close to them is unexpectedly in the
  hospital, injured, or missing; their child's school closed or has an
  emergency today; an evacuation or severe weather warning covers where
  they are.

These are not urgent, even when they are soon:

- A reminder of something the user already knows about that has not
  changed, such as a meeting on their calendar or a known flight.
- A change to plans that are a day or more away, a deadline, bill, or
  renewal that is not due today, or a package that is simply on its way.
- News, newsletters, promotions, recommendations, receipts, finished
  work, or general updates.
- An old message, or a worrying topic with no current event behind it.

Urgency comes from what the source facts establish, not from alarming
wording. When you cannot tell whether the change is current, whether the
user already knows, or whether it matters within hours, choose `high`.
